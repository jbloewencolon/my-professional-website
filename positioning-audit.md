# Positioning & Audience Audit — jordanloewencolon.com

*A read of what the site's language signals on its own: who shows up, why, and what they will try to hire this person to do. Every claim is grounded in quoted copy from the site as it currently reads. No goals or intended-audience brief were supplied, so Section 5 is treated as N/A and the recommendations aim at the strongest viable high-value segment.*

---

## Executive read

This site currently reads as **a scholar-practitioner's thought-leadership and speaking platform first, and a consultancy second.** The language that greets a visitor — *"Indigenous Taíno technologist,"* a Deleuze epigraph (*"The task of perception entails pulverizing the world…"*), and a *"Lineages"* list running from Glissant to Wynter to Deleuze — signals depth, politics, and a distinctive point of view, and it will reliably pull **keynote bookings, university invitations, Indigenous data-sovereignty collaborations, and podcast/press**. The one page written in plain buyer language, Speaking & Consulting, is genuinely strong (*"the talks a booker can actually put on a program,"* rates listed openly) and does most of the commercial work. But the most bankable, retainer-shaped offering — the **AI Alt Lab**, which *"helps governments, AI startups, and health organizations make their data practices ready for generative AI"* — exists only in the downloadable bio pack and `llms.txt`, and **appears nowhere on the actual site.** The net effect: the site will get hired mostly for *talks and ideas*, while the higher-margin *advisory and governance retainers* it is clearly qualified for leak away because a scanning buyer never sees a concrete "here is what you can hire me to do, and what you'll receive."

## 1) Language profile

**Register and vocabulary — a deliberate two-register blend.** The site code-switches, and it knows it does. One register is **critical-theory / decolonial-humanities**: *"data justice,"* *"digital extraction,"* *"the Right to Opacity,"* *"Empire 2.0,"* *"communities too often flattened by emerging technologies,"* plus a *"Lineages"* apparatus (*"Names and texts my work answers to"*). The other is **plain-business booking copy**: *"the talks a booker can actually put on a program,"* *"calibrated for rooms that contain both engineers and the people who pay them,"* *"Listed, because guessing wastes everyone's time."* Jargon density is **high on Home and About, low on Speaking and Contact.** Reading level is roughly graduate-seminar on the front pages (abstract nouns, theorist name-drops) dropping to crisp trade-press on the commercial pages.

**Tone and stance — authoritative, dry, and unusually candid about money and boundaries.** It is assertive, not hedged: *"A hype keynote. A doom keynote. A vendor for ethics-washing"* (a list of refusals), *"a standing voice in the room that is neither the optimist nor the doomer."* The voice is confident enough to publish rates and to say *"guessing wastes everyone's time."* It is provocative on politics (*"Universities are Doomed and the Humanities Can Save Them,"* *"digital colonialism"*) but never salesy — there is almost no hype vocabulary.

**Signature lexis and the worldview it signals.** Recurring terms and what each one tells a reader:
- *"Right to Opacity"* / *"extractable, or machine-readable"* → a resistance-to-datafication politics; privacy and refusal as values, not just compliance.
- *"Empire 2.0,"* *"digital extraction,"* *"AI extractivism,"* *"data colonialism"* → an anti-colonial, structural-critique frame; treats AI as a power question, not only a technical one.
- *"Indigenous data sovereignty,"* *"OCAP®,"* *"CARE Principles,"* *"Access-and-Benefit-Sharing"* → real, citable governance fluency in a specialized field, not gestural.
- *"a stream, not three buckets,"* *"one practice, not seven buckets"* → an integrationist self-concept: he resists being reduced to a single service line (which is intellectually honest and commercially costly — see §4).
- *"ensemble,"* *"model vs. system"* → a humanist-in-the-loop stance on the future of work.

**Point of view — who is "you"?** The site rarely addresses a "you" directly; it mostly speaks *about* the work in third/first person. Where a "you" does appear it is a **builder/governor of AI**: *"For teams building or governing AI systems who want a standing voice in the room,"* and consulting scoped to *"should we build this,"* *"how do we write a policy that survives the next model."* Cultural/political signaling is explicit and unmissable: a land acknowledgment, Taíno-language lines (*"guakía · Taíno · 'we, ours'"*, *"wo m'adan tokodo'aki ibakuaiba'ni"*), and a masthead that leads with *"Indigenous Taíno technologist"* **before** *"Responsible AI strategist."* Identity is placed ahead of function by design.

## 2) Positioning and value proposition

**Problems the language claims to solve.** Framed at two altitudes. High/abstract: preventing extraction and harm — *"communities too often flattened by emerging technologies,"* *"Preventing AI Extractivism."* Concrete/operational (but under-surfaced): *"how do we write a policy that survives the next model,"* *"what frameworks do we owe the community we are drawing data from,"* and — only in the bio pack — *"get their governance ready for generative AI before you deploy, not after."*

**What is implicitly for sale, and how legible it is.** Five things are on offer, in descending order of how *obvious* they are to a visitor:
1. **Keynotes and workshops** — unmistakable. Five named talks with runtimes and audiences, and a rate card.
2. **Advisory retainer** — stated, but abstract: *"Quarterly retainer. For teams building or governing AI systems."*
3. **Scoped consulting projects** — *"4–12 weeks,"* with good example questions.
4. **Thought leadership / the book** — *Reality Technologies* (Fortress Press), HBR, Springer.
5. **The AI Alt Lab consultancy** — the clearest, most buyer-legible offering… **and it is absent from the site.** It lives only in `bio-and-headshot-pack-…md` and `llms.txt`.

So: **what you can hire him to *speak* about is crystal clear; what you can hire his *practice* to *do and deliver* is only half-built on the page.** A scanning executive gets talks and a rate card, but no "engagement model," no named client logos for the advisory work (Utah, City of Berkeley never appear on-site), and no through-line from the essays to a service.

**Credibility markers and their framing.** Strong and varied: *Harvard Business Review*, Springer *AI & Society*, *Journal of the American Academy of Religion*; *"Tech Policy Fellow at the Aspen Institute";* teaching at Queen's Smith School of Business; the *StateScoop* piece on Utah; *"$2M+ supported in grant-funded research · 10,000+ downloads of Responsible AI media."* These are framed as **an integrated body of work** (*"different jobs, all the work"*), which reads as intellectually serious. The framing skews *academic-prestige* over *client-outcome*: it proves he is credible and published, more than it proves engagements shipped results.

**Call to action / friction.** Low-friction and consistent — *"put fifteen minutes on the calendar,"* a Calendly link repeated on Home, Speaking, Contact, and Footer, plus a downloadable press pack. One quiet friction point: the contact address is **`jbl6@queensu.ca`, a university email**, which subtly codes the practice as academic-adjacent rather than a standing firm — a small dissonance for a buyer about to discuss a paid retainer.

## 3) Audience attraction — the core deliverable

Each segment below names the language cues that pull it, the engagement it would pursue, likely scale, and the strength/confidence of the pull.

### A. Conference & corporate event organizers / bookers (keynotes) — Pull: **High** · Confidence: **High**
- **Who:** Program chairs, comms/brand teams, summit and offsite organizers in tech, data, and business-leadership events.
- **Cues:** *"the talks a booker can actually put on a program… workshopped on stages from Berlin to Toronto";* corporate-legible titles — *"Doing AI Differently"* (*"How orgs can build with AI while staying human"*), *"Human-AI Ensembles"* (*"The future of work beyond automation and replacement"*); an open rate card (*"Keynote $5–15k"*). Each cue says *bookable, road-tested, priced.*
- **Engagement:** Keynote or workshop.
- **Scale/cycle:** $5–15k domestic; short cycle (a booker can decide from this page in one sitting).
- **Why High/High:** This is the page the site is *best* at; it removes the usual guesswork.

### B. Universities, humanities programs, academic convenings — Pull: **High** · Confidence: **High**
- **Who:** Departments, humanities centers, endowed-lecture committees, conference units (e.g., AAR).
- **Cues:** *"Artificial Opportunity: Universities are Doomed and the Humanities Can Save Them"* (Syracuse Tolley keynote); the *"Lineages"* list; peer-reviewed venues; *"Honoraria for universities… negotiated separately and gladly."*
- **Engagement:** Invited keynote, guest lecture, teaching, research collaboration.
- **Scale/cycle:** Honorarium-scale (often < $5k); committee-paced, seasonal.
- **Why High/High:** The register *is* the academy's register; this audience feels directly addressed.

### C. Indigenous-led organizations & data-sovereignty initiatives — Pull: **High** · Confidence: **High**
- **Who:** Tribal/Indigenous governance bodies, data-sovereignty networks, allied NGOs and funders.
- **Cues:** *"Indigenous data justice,"* *"OCAP®,"* *"CARE Principles,"* *"Data Res Nullius: The Doctrine of Discovery in the Age of AI"* (US Indigenous Data Sovereignty & Governance Summit), the Taíno-language footer, *"Indigenous-led work… negotiated separately and gladly."*
- **Engagement:** Advisory, research/grant collaboration, honoraria-adjusted speaking.
- **Scale/cycle:** Grant- and honorarium-scale; relationship- and trust-paced.
- **Why High/High:** The commitment is specific and demonstrated, not gestural — this is a genuine moat.

### D. Government / public-sector AI governance (state & local) — Pull: **Medium-High potential, Medium as-written** · Confidence: **Medium**
- **Who:** State/municipal policy leads, public-sector innovation offices, govtech advisors.
- **Cues on-site:** *"Tech Policy Fellow at the Aspen Institute,"* the *StateScoop* Utah piece, *"how do we write a policy that survives the next model."* **Cues off-site (bio pack only):** *"partners with state and local governments — including Utah and the City of Berkeley."*
- **Engagement:** Advisory retainer, scoped policy/governance consulting, advisory-committee seat.
- **Scale/cycle:** Retainer or mid-five-figure project; slow, procurement-shaped.
- **Why only Medium as-written:** The proof that would close this buyer (named government clients, the AI Alt Lab) **is not on the site.** The credentials imply capability; the page never states the offer. High-value, under-served.

### E. AI startups / scale-ups needing responsible-AI credibility — Pull: **Medium** · Confidence: **Medium**
- **Who:** Founders, heads of product/policy at AI companies wanting governance before scale.
- **Cues:** *"For teams building or governing AI systems";* *"Ethics in Entrepreneurship"* (Data Natives × EIE); Founder Institute; affiliations with *Candidly AI* and *Nera Lake.*
- **Engagement:** Fractional/advisory retainer, workshop, "should we build this" project.
- **Scale/cycle:** Retainer or workshop; founder-fast when the fit is felt.
- **Why Medium:** The signals are real but scattered, and the two company affiliations are **unexplained** — a visitor can't tell whether he advises, cofounded, or sits on the board of *Candidly AI* / *Nera Lake*, which mutes the proof.

### F. Digital-health & health-innovation organizations — Pull: **Medium (bio pack) / Low (on-site)** · Confidence: **Medium/Speculative**
- **Who:** Health-innovation centers, digital-therapeutics teams, clinical-AI groups.
- **Cues:** Supernova Immersives (*"AI-augmented virtual reality for mental health"*), the Digital Cancer Twin Project, *"How, Why, Should We Digital Twin?,"* *"health organizations"* (bio pack).
- **Engagement:** Advisory, workshop, research collaboration.
- **Scale/cycle:** Project/retainer; committee-paced.
- **Why mixed:** Health is a genuine track record, but the site frames it mostly as **past ventures and talks**, not a present offer — no "I help health teams do X."

### G. Media, podcasts, journalists — Pull: **Medium-High** · Confidence: **High**
- **Who:** Producers, hosts, reporters covering AI ethics/policy.
- **Cues:** A dedicated Press page, *"Pitch a show,"* the downloadable bio & headshot pack, HBR and *The Conversation* bylines.
- **Engagement:** Commentary, interviews (usually unpaid; reputation/funnel value).
- **Scale/cycle:** No fee, fast; feeds Segments A–D.

### H. Book / literary-festival & publisher orbit — Pull: **Low, emerging** · Confidence: **Speculative**
- **Who:** Festival programmers, publisher publicity, ideas-conference bookers.
- **Cues:** *Reality Technologies* (Fortress Press, forthcoming), *"al colibrí"* poetry practice.
- **Engagement:** Book talks, panels, launch-driven press — activates on publication.

## 4) Who it repels or misses

**Deliberate, healthy filtering (keep it):**
- **Efficiency-maximization / automation buyers.** *"The future of work beyond automation and replacement"* and *"AI tools… can erode trust, creativity and agency"* actively wave off anyone shopping for "AI to cut headcount." Good — that's not the practice.
- **Ethics-washing buyers.** *"A vendor for ethics-washing"* named as a refusal. This repels the exact client who would waste his time, and reassures the one who won't.
- **Extractive / surveillance / defense AI.** *"Empire 2.0,"* *"biometric data… absorbed into AI systems,"* *"digital colonialism"* make this a hard no. Deliberate and on-brand.

**Unintended loss (this is where good-fit money leaks):**
- **The compliance-driven governance buyer.** A GC, Chief Risk/AI Officer, or procurement lead scanning for regulatory readiness will not find their vocabulary anywhere: no *AI Act, NIST AI RMF, ISO 42001, audit, risk assessment, model documentation.* The nearest phrase, *"a policy that survives the next model,"* reads as wisdom, not a deliverable. This is a large, well-funded buyer the credentials clearly qualify him for — and the language never reaches out to them.
- **The pragmatic executive who bounces before the good page.** The first impression (Deleuze epigraph, *"Lineages,"* *"pulverizing the world"*) codes *professor*. A pragmatic VP may conclude "brilliant academic, not a consultant" and leave **before reaching Speaking & Consulting**, which is the page that would have converted them. The strongest commercial copy is buried two clicks deep behind the most academic copy.
- **Anyone looking for the AI Alt Lab.** The practice with the most legible offer and the best client logos **cannot be found on the site.** A referral who was told "check out his lab" lands and sees essays and talks, not the lab. Pure leakage.
- **"Which single thing do you do?" buyers.** *"A stream, not three buckets… one practice, not seven buckets"* is intellectually honest but asks the buyer to do the synthesis. Time-poor buyers want one clear lane; the integrationist framing can read as unfocused to them.

**Small credibility drags for a premium buyer.** The About page's H1 contains a typo — *"New worlds recquire new ways of seeing"* — and the Supernova paragraph has a comma-splice fragment (*"…Internal Family Systems Therapy, entered the Founder Institute accelerator…"*). Minor individually; against a $5–15k+ positioning they nick the "senior advisor" signal.

## 6) Recommendations (prioritized, with rewrites in the site's voice)

The through-line: **do not sand off the Taíno / critical-theory voice — it is the moat.** Generic "responsible AI consultants" are interchangeable; the data-sovereignty depth is why he is bookable at a premium and un-clonable by competitors. The fixes *add a concrete "what you can hire me to do" layer on top of* the distinctive voice, and surface the practice that already exists.

**1. Put the AI Alt Lab on the site — this is the highest-impact change.** Its absence is the single biggest gap. Add a short "Practice" block on Home and a section on Speaking/About, pulling the proof that currently hides in the bio pack.
> **Before (Home, current):** *"Indigenous Taíno technologist / Responsible AI strategist"* — then straight to a Deleuze epigraph.
> **After (add directly beneath the subtitle):** *"Through the **AI Alt Lab**, I help governments, AI startups, and health teams get their data and AI governance ready for generative AI — before they ship, not after. Recent partners include the State of Utah and the City of Berkeley."*

**2. Give the pragmatic buyer one plain-English line above the theory.** Keep the epigraph — just don't let it be the *first* thing that defines the offer. Add a single functional sentence between the masthead and the epigraph.
> **Before:** name → subtitle → *"The task of perception entails pulverizing the world…"*
> **After (insert one line):** *"I help organizations build AI practices they can stand behind — and make the hard trade-offs concrete enough to decide."* (This line already exists, verbatim, in your LinkedIn "About" in the bio pack: *"I help organizations build AI practices they can stand behind."* Promote it to the homepage.)

**3. Make the Advisory offer concrete for the governance/compliance buyer — without adopting compliance-vendor voice.** Right now *"a standing voice in the room that is neither the optimist nor the doomer"* sells a *posture*; add the *deliverables*.
> **Before (Speaking → Advisory):** *"Quarterly retainer. For teams building or governing AI systems who want a standing voice in the room that is neither the optimist nor the doomer."*
> **After:** *"Quarterly retainer for teams building or governing AI systems. A standing voice that is neither the optimist nor the doomer — plus the work that voice produces: data-governance and AI-use policies your legal and product teams can actually apply, a review cadence for new models and vendors, and a defensible position on the data you draw from communities. Built to satisfy scrutiny (AI Act, NIST AI RMF, procurement) without collapsing into checkbox ethics."*
> *(One clause names the standards the compliance buyer searches for; the last clause keeps your anti-ethics-washing stance intact.)*

**4. Explain the company affiliations.** *Candidly AI* and *Nera Lake* currently sit as unlabeled logos.
> **After (one line under the affiliations grid):** *"Advisor to Candidly AI and Nera Lake on responsible data and AI practice."* (Adjust the verb to the true relationship — the point is that an unexplained logo proves nothing.)

**5. Add a light, buyer-facing "What you can hire me to do" summary — reconcile it with the "stream, not buckets" idea rather than contradicting it.**
> **After (Speaking or a short Services strip):** *"One practice, four ways in: a **talk** for the room that needs to think differently; a **workshop** for the team about to ship; an **advisory retainer** for the group governing AI over time; a **scoped project** for the single hard question — 'should we build this,' 'what do we owe the community whose data we use,' 'what policy survives the next model.'"* (Keeps *"a stream, not three buckets"* as philosophy while handing the buyer the lane they need.)

**6. Fix the credibility nicks.** Correct the About H1 typo *"recquire" → "require,"* and repair the Supernova sentence fragment. At this price point, headline copy has to be clean.

**7. Consider a practice-appropriate contact address.** *`jbl6@queensu.ca`* codes "academic." A firm-branded or personal-domain address (e.g., on the AI Alt Lab or jordanloewencolon.com domain) would better match a paid-retainer conversation, without removing the university affiliation elsewhere.

**Sequencing:** #1 and #2 change *who converts* and should ship first; #3 and #5 change *what they buy* (talks → retainers); #4, #6, #7 are quick credibility polish.

### Bottom line
The writing earns *authority, distinctiveness, and trust* and will keep the **talk / teach / collaborate** funnel (A, B, C, G) full with little effort. The **advisory and governance money (D, E, F)** — retainers, scoped projects, the AI Alt Lab — is the underperforming asset, not because the credentials are missing but because **the offer is unstated and the practice is invisible on the page.** Surface the AI Alt Lab, give the pragmatic buyer one plain sentence before the theory, and make the advisory deliverables concrete, and the same voice that currently sells ideas will start selling engagements.
